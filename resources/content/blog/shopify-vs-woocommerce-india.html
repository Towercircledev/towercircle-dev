If you're planning to sell online in India, the first big decision is the platform. The two names you'll hear most are Shopify and WooCommerce. Both power lakhs of stores, both can take UPI and cards, and both can look beautiful. But they suit very different businesses.

We've built and managed stores on both. Here's an honest, practical comparison for Indian sellers — no jargon.

The short answer

  • Choose Shopify if you want to start selling quickly, don't want to manage hosting or security, and prefer a predictable monthly cost.
  • Choose WooCommerce if you already have a WordPress website with good Google traffic, need deep customisation, or want to avoid a monthly platform fee and are comfortable maintaining it.

For most first-time D2C brands in India, Shopify is the safer choice. For content-heavy businesses that already rank on Google with WordPress, WooCommerce can make sense.

1. Setup and ease of use

Shopify is a hosted platform. You sign up, choose a theme, add products and connect payments — there's no server, no software updates and no plugins breaking after an update. The admin is clean and works well on a phone app, which matters when you're packing orders and replying to customers at the same time.

WooCommerce is a free plugin for WordPress. You need hosting, a domain, WordPress installed, a theme and several plugins. It's flexible, but someone has to keep WordPress, the theme and every plugin updated — or the site slows down and becomes a security risk.

2. Cost: monthly fee vs "free"

Shopify has a monthly subscription (check Shopify's current pricing for India, as plans change), plus paid apps and sometimes a paid theme. WooCommerce itself is free, but you pay for hosting, premium plugins, a theme and — very often — a developer to maintain it.

In our experience, the real cost difference is smaller than it looks. A cheap WooCommerce setup on low-quality shared hosting usually ends up slow, and a slow store costs you sales. Compare total cost over a year — platform, hosting, apps/plugins and maintenance — not just the sticker price.

3. Payments, COD and shipping in India

Both platforms work well with Indian payment gateways like Razorpay, PayU and Cashfree, which support UPI, cards, net banking and wallets. Both also support Cash on Delivery, with rules such as COD limits or extra charges.

For shipping, both integrate with aggregators like Shiprocket, which give you multiple courier partners, automatic AWB generation and tracking. Shopify's integrations are usually quicker to set up; WooCommerce integrations work but may need more configuration.

4. Speed and performance

Speed directly affects sales, especially for shoppers on mobile data. Shopify stores run on Shopify's global infrastructure, so a well-built store is reliably fast — as long as you don't overload it with apps.

WooCommerce speed depends entirely on your hosting and how the site is built. On good cloud hosting with caching, it can be very fast. On cheap shared hosting with many plugins, it can be painfully slow.

5. SEO and content

WordPress is excellent for blogging and content, and WooCommerce inherits that strength. If your business depends on ranking for many informational searches (guides, recipes, how-tos), WooCommerce has an edge.

Shopify handles product and collection SEO well — editable titles, descriptions, clean URLs and automatic sitemaps — and has a built-in blog that's good enough for most stores. For product-focused brands, Shopify SEO is more than sufficient.

6. Security and maintenance

With Shopify, security, SSL, backups of the platform and PCI compliance for payments are handled for you. With WooCommerce, they're your responsibility (or your developer's): updates, backups, malware scans and firewall settings.

If you don't have a technical person or a maintenance plan, this point alone often decides it in Shopify's favour.

7. Customisation and flexibility

WooCommerce is open-source, so almost anything is possible with code — custom checkout logic, unusual pricing rules, B2B portals. Shopify is customisable through themes, apps and its developer tools, but some deep changes (especially to checkout) are limited on lower plans.

If your business model is truly unusual — wholesale price lists, bookings, subscriptions with special rules — you might need either heavy WooCommerce customisation or a custom e-commerce website.

Comparison at a glance

  • Fastest to launch: Shopify
  • Lowest maintenance: Shopify
  • Best for blogging/content SEO: WooCommerce
  • Most customisable: WooCommerce
  • Predictable monthly cost: Shopify
  • Indian payments, COD and shipping: both work well

Our recommendation

For a new brand selling physical products — clothing, jewellery, beauty, home décor, food — we usually recommend Shopify. You launch faster, spend your time on marketing instead of maintenance, and the store stays fast and secure.

If you already run a WordPress site with steady Google traffic, adding WooCommerce can be the smarter move — just budget for proper hosting and maintenance.

Frequently asked questions

Can I move from WooCommerce to Shopify later?

Yes. Products, customers and order history can be migrated, and redirects keep your Google rankings. Plan it properly to avoid broken links.

Does Shopify support GST invoices?

Yes. You can set up taxes and use apps to generate GST-compliant invoices automatically.

Which is better for Instagram sellers?

Shopify, in most cases. It connects to Instagram and Facebook shops easily and is simple to manage from a phone.
