When a business outgrows Excel, the next question is: should we buy ready-made software — like Zoho, Tally, Salesforce or a ready HRMS — or build custom software around our own process? Both are valid choices. The right answer depends on your process, team size, budget and growth plans.

Here's a practical comparison to help you decide, based on what we see working with Indian SMEs.

What "ready-made" and "custom" really mean

Ready-made (off-the-shelf / SaaS) software is built for thousands of businesses. You subscribe, configure it, and adapt your process to how the software works.

Custom software is built for your business. It follows your process, your fields and your reports — and you own it.

When ready-made software is the better choice

  • Your process is fairly standard (basic accounting, simple invoicing, standard payroll)
  • You need something working this week
  • You have a small team and a small budget
  • Compliance features (like statutory accounting) are the main need

For example, accounting and statutory compliance are usually best handled by established accounting software. There's no reason to reinvent them.

When custom software is the better choice

  • Your process is your advantage — and generic software forces you to change it
  • You're paying for several tools that don't talk to each other
  • Per-user monthly fees grow painfully as you hire
  • You need specific approvals, calculations or reports no tool provides
  • You want customers or vendors to log in to a portal branded as yours
  • You want full control and ownership of your data

Cost: subscription vs ownership

Ready-made software has a low start cost but ongoing per-user fees, plus add-ons and often paid customisation. Custom software costs more upfront, then mainly hosting and support.

Over three to five years, the total cost can be closer than it looks — especially for teams with many users. Compare total cost of ownership, not just the first-year price.

Time to start

Ready-made wins here: you can sign up today. Custom software takes weeks — though building in phases means the most important module can go live early (often within 3–6 weeks), with more modules added later.

Fit and adoption

The best software is the one your team actually uses. Ready-made tools often have many features nobody needs and miss the few that matter, so teams fall back to Excel. Custom software shows each person only what they need, which usually means faster adoption.

Data ownership and security

With SaaS, your data lives on the provider's servers under their terms. With custom software hosted on your own server or cloud account, you control access, backups and where data is stored. Either can be secure — what matters is good access control, encryption, backups and audit logs.

Integrations

Many businesses end up with a CRM, an HR tool, a billing tool and spreadsheets — none connected. Custom software can combine these into one system, or connect your existing tools (website, payment gateway, WhatsApp, email, Tally exports) so data flows automatically.

The hybrid approach (often the smartest)

You don't have to choose one or the other for everything. A common, cost-effective setup:

  • Accounting: keep your existing accounting software
  • Operations: custom software for your unique workflow — CRM, orders, projects, bookings or inventory
  • Connection: export data in a format your accountant can import

Questions to ask before deciding

  1. Which tasks take the most time today, and why?
  2. Can a ready-made tool do 90% of what we need without awkward workarounds?
  3. How many users will we have in two years?
  4. What reports do we wish we had?
  5. Who will own and maintain the system?

How a good custom software project should run

  • Workflow mapping with the people who'll use it
  • A clickable prototype before coding
  • Phased delivery — the most valuable module first
  • Role-based access, backups and activity logs
  • Training, data import and ongoing support
  • Source code and data owned by you

Frequently asked questions

Is custom software only for large companies?

No. Small and mid-sized businesses often benefit most, because a focused tool can save hours every day.

Can custom software work on mobile?

Yes. Modern web applications work in any browser, on phones and laptops, without installing an app.

What if we outgrow it?

Because it's built for you, new modules and features can be added as you grow.
