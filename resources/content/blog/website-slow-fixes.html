A slow website quietly loses customers. Visitors on mobile data won't wait — if your page takes too long, they press back and choose a competitor. Google also uses page experience as a ranking signal, so a slow site can rank lower too.

The good news: most slow websites suffer from the same handful of problems. Here are the 8 fixes that make the biggest difference, in the order we usually tackle them.

First, measure your speed

Open Google PageSpeed Insights and test your homepage and one important inner page (a service or product page). Look at the mobile score and the Core Web Vitals:

  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint): how long the main content takes to appear
  • INP (Interaction to Next Paint): how quickly the page responds to taps and clicks
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift): how much the page jumps around while loading

Save the results so you can compare after making changes.

1. Compress and resize your images

This is the number-one cause of slow websites. A photo straight from a phone or camera can be several megabytes; on a website it should usually be a small fraction of that.

  • Resize images to the size they're actually displayed (rarely wider than 1,920 px).
  • Convert to modern formats like WebP.
  • Use lazy loading so images below the fold load only when needed.

2. Upgrade cheap shared hosting

If your server is slow to respond, nothing else fixes it. Cheap shared hosting puts hundreds of websites on one server. Moving to good cloud hosting — ideally with servers in India if most visitors are here — can transform speed.

3. Remove unnecessary plugins and apps

Every WordPress plugin or Shopify app can add scripts and styles to every page. Audit them: remove what you don't use, and replace heavy plugins with lighter alternatives. Many sites we audit have sliders, pop-ups and social widgets nobody needs.

4. Turn on caching

Caching stores ready-made versions of your pages so the server doesn't rebuild them for every visitor. On WordPress, a good caching plugin plus server-level caching makes a big difference. Also make sure browser caching is enabled for images, CSS and JavaScript.

5. Reduce heavy fonts and scripts

  • Use one or two font families with only the weights you need, and self-host them where possible.
  • Delay non-essential scripts (chat widgets, heatmaps) until after the page loads.
  • Avoid loading entire libraries like jQuery just for a small effect.

6. Avoid large sliders and videos at the top

Homepage sliders with five large images are slow and rarely clicked. A single, well-optimised hero image with a clear message loads faster and converts better. If you need video, use a lightweight thumbnail that loads the player only when clicked.

7. Fix layout shifts

If text jumps down as images or ads load, visitors tap the wrong thing — and Google notices. Always set width and height for images, and reserve space for banners and embeds.

8. Use a CDN and compression

A Content Delivery Network (CDN) serves your files from locations closer to visitors. Combined with gzip or Brotli compression on the server, it reduces the amount of data each visitor downloads.

What results to expect

For a typical small business site, fixing images, hosting and plugins alone often cuts load time dramatically. The aim is a page that shows its main content in about two seconds on a mobile connection, with good Core Web Vitals.

Frequently asked questions

What's a good PageSpeed score?

Aim for 90+ on mobile, but focus on real Core Web Vitals passing rather than the score alone.

Will speed changes break my site?

They can if done carelessly. Always take a backup and test after each change.

Is Shopify faster than WordPress?

A well-built site on either can be fast. Shopify handles hosting for you; WordPress speed depends on your hosting and setup.
